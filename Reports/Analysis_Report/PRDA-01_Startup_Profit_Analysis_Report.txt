PRDA-01 STARTUP PROFIT ANALYSIS REPORT

Executive Summary
This project analyzes investment spending and Profit using 50 startups. The aim is to explore relationships between R&D Spend, Administration, Marketing Spend and Profit, compare state averages, and fit a multiple linear regression model.

Dataset
50 records, fields: R&D Spend, Administration, Marketing Spend, State, Profit.

Key metrics
Total Profit: $5,600,631.96
Average Profit: $112,012.64
Total R&D Spend: $3,686,080.78
R&D Spend–Profit correlation: 0.9729
Florida average Profit: $118,774.02

Regression model
Profit = 50,122.19299 + (0.805715050 × R&D Spend) + (-0.026815968 × Administration) + (0.027228065 × Marketing Spend)

Model performance
R²: 0.950745994 (95.07%)
MAE: $6,471.45040
MSE: 78,417,126.01913
RMSE: $8,855.34449

Insights & Recommendations
- R&D Spend has the strongest positive correlation with Profit in this sample; explore investment opportunities and monitor ROI.
- Florida has the highest average Profit in the dataset; investigate context before drawing conclusions.
- Monitor spending efficiency; correlation is not causation.
- Validate the regression model on unseen data before using it for decisions.

Limitations
- Only 50 observations.
- Correlation does not prove causality.
- Model fit on the sample does not guarantee generalization.
- Additional business context and validation are needed.

Conclusion
R&D Spend is the strongest spending-related correlate of Profit in this sample. State-level profit differences and regression results provide exploratory insights, not causal or guaranteed predictive conclusions.
